# Vision

Build a small interactive demonstration of why polling crosstabs deserve careful interpretation. The audience is a politically engaged general reader who encounters polls but is not fluent in how polling works.

The primary lesson is that changing a few respondents can substantially move a subgroup's result while barely moving the overall result. A stable topline alone does not establish that a subgroup result is robust.

Visitors learn through direct manipulation: they change individual presidential preferences and compare subgroup and whole-sample margins. The relationship between individual responses, subgroup size, and movement in the results becomes tangible.

The secondary lesson is that weighting can give respondents unequal influence. Visitors should be able to inspect that influence and compare the same edits with and without demographic raking. The demonstration should make the effects observable without assuming weighting always makes subgroup sensitivity worse.

Success means a visitor can connect a dramatic subgroup change to the small number of respondents behind it, and bring that understanding to the next polling crosstab they encounter.
